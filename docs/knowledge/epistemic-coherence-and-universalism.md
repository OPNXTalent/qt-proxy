# Prism knowledge resource: Epistemic Coherence and Model Humility

Status: **Proposed / review required**. This document is a source resource, not a live system prompt or a deployed retrieval entry.

## Purpose
Support open-ended theological inquiry about universal reconciliation, divine goodness, judgment, free will, the problem of evil, and limits of finite reasoning. Do not force these topics into unrelated answers.

## Core principle
An explanatory model may be internally coherent yet incomplete. Necessity *within* a model is not automatically necessity *in reality*. Conclusions must remain accountable to the full relevant scriptural witness, with clear separation of (a) what a passage states, (b) contextual interpretation, (c) philosophical inference, and (d) unresolved questions.

RCT is an interpretive framework, not an infallible authority. It is designed for **principled elasticity**: revise provisional claims when warranted, while accepting that decisive contrary evidence can falsify a claim or undermine the framework itself. Never redefine evidence simply to rescue RCT.

## Method for user conversations
1. Identify the actual question and the level of certainty being claimed: possibility, hope, plausibility, doctrinal conclusion, or metaphysical necessity.
2. Present the strongest relevant argument fairly, including named thinkers without caricature.
3. Examine supporting and challenging scriptural texts in their literary, historical, and linguistic contexts. Do not treat either group as disposable.
4. Identify hidden premises, including assumptions about goodness, justice, freedom, time, and the finality or purpose of judgment.
5. Distinguish a valid inference from adequately justified premises. A competing *conceivable* account challenges claims of necessity only if it is genuinely coherent with the established evidence.
6. Where tensions remain, say so without manufacturing certainty. Invite the user to examine counterarguments and allow their objections to change the analysis.
7. Explain RCT's proposed lens (relational coherence/decoherence, autonomy, YHWH as source of life) as a model, not as explicit biblical vocabulary.
8. Avoid treating a person's religious tradition as a reason to dismiss their question.

## Topic resource: Universalism and metaphysical necessity
A strong formulation of David Bentley Hart's view: God is the Good itself; rational creatures are oriented toward the Good; evil choice reflects ignorance or disordered desire; perfected rational freedom ultimately chooses God; thus final reconciliation is necessary. Hart does **not** simply teach torture or forced confession as the mechanism of salvation.

Key tests:
- Does divine goodness *necessarily* exclude irreversible relational loss? What warrants that premise?
- Does perfected rational freedom *necessarily* imply acceptance of God? Does genuine freedom require enduring alternative possibilities?
- Does a guaranteed outcome logically imply coercion? Not by itself; articulate the competing accounts of freedom.
- Does a passage about God's desire for salvation establish the fulfillment of that desire for every individual?
- Are descriptions of destruction, punishment, exclusion, and judgment necessarily temporary, final, or context-dependent?
- Do not assert that the presence of alternative interpretations automatically refutes universalism; it challenges *exclusive necessity* when those alternatives remain defensible.

Relevant texts to examine, without proof-texting:
- Reconciliation: Romans 5:18; 1 Corinthians 15:22–28; Colossians 1:20; 1 Timothy 2:4; Philippians 2:10–11.
- Judgment: Daniel 12:2; Matthew 7:13–14, 7:23, 10:28, 25:46; Mark 9:43–48; John 15:5–6; Revelation 20:11–15.
- Relational language: John 3:19–21; Romans 1:24–28; Romans 6:23; James 1:14–15.

Do not assume that every judgment is restorative; do not assume every judgment is irreversible. Explain disputes about *aiōnios*, *‘olam*, destruction, and reconciliation when relevant.

## Related topic: The problem of evil
Shared questions: what does divine goodness require, what does creaturely autonomy permit, and how can finite observers assess eternal purposes? RCT can explore evil as relational disorder, but that does not itself answer why natural suffering occurs or why YHWH permits severe harm to innocents. Do not trivialize suffering by invoking unknown divine reasons. Universal restoration, even if true, does not automatically explain the justification for each instance of suffering.

## Epistemic analogy
Classical physics remains useful within its domain, while quantum mechanics exposed limits of applying some classical assumptions universally. Use this as an analogy for model scope, **not** as proof of theology, the falsity of materialism, or the existence of a distinct divine logic. Logical validity is distinct from premise adequacy; omniscience may involve an informational and relational perspective inaccessible to finite observers. Whether understanding expands after death is a theological hope, not demonstrated access to exhaustive divine knowledge (cf. 1 Corinthians 13:12).

## Conversation examples
- “Does Hart prove that a good God must save everyone?” → Explain his argument, distinguish necessity from possibility, test premises and scripture, invite challenge.
- “If God knows every evil, why allow it?” → Discuss the limits and strengths of free-will and relational accounts; explicitly confront natural evil and innocent suffering.
- “Does Matthew 25:46 disprove universalism?” → Compare duration and semantic interpretations, the antithetical parallel, and relevant reconciliation passages; acknowledge dispute.
- “Is RCT unfalsifiable?” → No: it allows principled revision *and* genuine failure thresholds. Ask what evidence would require revising or abandoning a given claim.

- Faithfully represents Hart and competing universalist, conditionalist, and traditionalist readings.
- Distinguishes scripture, exegesis, model-dependent inference, and uncertainty.
- Does not smuggle predetermined anti-universalist answers into neutral questions.
- Can concede an RCT weakness when warranted.
- Retrieves only for relevant user questions; no unsolicited universalism debate.
