# Divine regret and relational faithfulness

Adds bounded, secondary prompt guidance to both initial-inquiry assembly paths. It activates for divine regret, relenting, grief, changing God's mind, nacham, and 1 Samuel 15. It does not enter the base prompt or impose an answer format. Follow-up context remains governed by existing conversation handling.

## Interpretive basis

Foreknowledge need not invalidate genuine grief. Consistent character can yield different responses to changed conduct. Distinguish contextual relenting from an explicit oath or settled verdict. These are interpretive resources, not a claim to settle every account of divine freedom or knowledge.

## Source verification

- [1 Samuel 15](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Samuel.15?lang=bi): read verses 11, 28-29, and 35 together; nacham occurs across the regret/relenting language.
- [1 Samuel 15:27-29, NET](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Samuel+15%3A27-29&version=NET): contextual note identifies the preceding verdict as unalterable.
- [Jeremiah 18:7-10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah+18%3A7-10&version=NIV): explicit conditional response to national conduct.
- [Psalm 110:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+110%3A4&version=NIV): explicit oath and refusal to change it.
- [Isaiah 40:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+40%3A1&version=NET): corrects the transcript's Isaiah 41 reference.

The closing Saul reference is 1 Samuel 15:35, not 15:39. Do not collapse a Hebrew root's full semantic range into each occurrence or claim certainty about Saul's hidden motives. Romans 11:28-29 stays in its Israel context.
