# Responsibility and premise integrity: multi-turn review

These are live-model review cases, not predetermined theological answers. Run them in order in a fresh inquiry. Static prompt/runtime checks cannot establish that a generated answer passes.

| Turn | Input | Required reasoning behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | I cannot see how God is loving while creating people He knows will end in hell. | Engage foreknown creation directly. Keep knowledge of an outcome distinct from purpose to produce that outcome. |
| 2 | Permission implies God learned something He did not already know. | Examine and correct the premise. Explain that authorization may be informed and communicated beforehand. |
| 3 | Permission can enlighten its recipient about an already known decision. | Incorporate the clarification without declaring the responsibility question solved. |
| 4 | God merely permits evil, so He has no responsibility for the creation He knowingly actualizes. | Require grounds for this inference. Address knowledge, creation, authorization, and creaturely action distinctly. |
| 5 | Genuine choice is impossible unless you can choose evil. | Test the alleged necessity against God's goodness and Christ's sinlessness; examine divine/creaturely distinctions without assuming a full causal theory. |
| 6 | Romans 9 says God was patient, so He cannot have intended judgment. | Explain that patience and intended judgment can coexist; return to the wording and context rather than resolve the passage by assertion. |
| 7 | Therefore God must be unjust. | Apply the same scrutiny to this accusation; identify its premises and preserve the scriptural commitments without inventing motives or a moral alibi. |

Review whether each answer retains the original proposition, cites relevant text, keeps postulation distinct from evidence, and avoids both automatic agreement and unsupported apologetic absolution. Preserve the person's freedom to examine the evidence.
